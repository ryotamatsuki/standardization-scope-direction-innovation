## 1. Executive freeze-decision verdict

`GO TO FULL PAPER`

The hostile post-Stage-10 audit materially narrows the paper's claim set, but it does not eliminate the paper's general economic mechanism or its central policy theorem. The quadratic baseline still delivers a verified and globally characterized threshold result: when standardization scope raises the transferability of common-layer innovation, firms privately reallocate a fixed R&D portfolio toward proprietary innovation, and sufficiently strong product-market rivalry makes the regulator prefer a unique interior scope even though complete scope is optimal under fixed positive symmetric R&D and under a coordinated symmetric-R&D benchmark with decentralized Bertrand pricing.

The paper should therefore remain on the full-paper route, but it must no longer be sold as establishing an unconstrained first-best comparison or generic robustness of policy-objective concavity. The exact selective-standardization theorem is a quadratic-baseline theorem; the broader technology result is the directional allocation mechanism only.

## 2. Mechanism card

| Element | Repaired canonical statement |
| --- | --- |
| Phenomenon | Broader technical standards make innovations in the common layer usable by a larger part of the rival product system. |
| Friction | The innovating firm does not internalize the rival-side benefit of common-layer innovation and, under product-market rivalry, treats that transfer as a competitive cost. |
| Strategic response | As scope expands, firms reduce the private relative allocation to common-layer R&D and shift a fixed R&D portfolio toward proprietary innovation. |
| Equilibrium effect | The direct quality gain from broader transferability is progressively offset by an endogenous retreat from common-layer innovation. |
| Welfare effect | With fixed positive symmetric R&D, broader scope is beneficial. With endogenous private R&D composition, sufficiently strong rivalry makes welfare strictly concave in scope in the quadratic baseline and yields a unique interior optimum. |
| Empirical implication | Expansions in standard scope should reduce the share of innovation directed toward standardized/common technological classes, with a stronger response where downstream products are closer substitutes. |

The mechanism can be stated without model notation: **standardization improves diffusion but weakens private appropriability of common innovation; firms respond by moving scarce innovative effort into proprietary activities, so a regulator with only the scope instrument may deliberately stop short of complete standardization.**

## 3. Minimal causal / strategic chain

`standardization scope ↑`

→ transferability of common-layer improvements ↑

→ common R&D benefits rivals more strongly

→ private relative return to common R&D ↓

→ firms reallocate fixed R&D toward proprietary innovation

→ the direct diffusion gain is partly offset by an innovation-composition loss

→ when rivalry is strong enough, complete standardization ceases to maximize decentralized welfare

→ unique selective scope in the quadratic baseline.

This is a strategic feedback mechanism, not a relabeling of a standard compatibility comparative static.

## 4. Essential versus tractability assumptions

### Essential for the proved policy theorem

1. **A common and a proprietary innovation margin.** Scope must differentially affect the cross-firm usability of common innovation.
2. **A private appropriability penalty generated by rivalry.** A firm's common innovation must improve the rival's competitive position; without this channel the portfolio response driving the second-best result disappears.
3. **Scope is chosen before private R&D composition.** The policy must alter firms' subsequent innovation incentives.
4. **Fixed total R&D capacity in the baseline.** The exact selective-standardization theorem currently isolates and proves a composition effect under `x_i+z_i=E`. Endogenous total R&D policy persistence is not proved.
5. **Quadratic innovation technology for the exact global scope theorem currently claimed.** The polynomial threshold, global strict concavity of the reduced policy objective, and uniqueness of the interior policy are verified for this baseline specification.
6. **Sufficient product-market rivalry.** The interior solution is not universal; it occurs iff the rivalry parameter crosses the verified threshold inside the frozen region.

### Tractability / normalization assumptions

1. Symmetric firms and the symmetric equilibrium presentation.
2. Linear-quadratic differentiated demand.
3. Differentiated Bertrand competition as the continuation game.
4. Zero marginal production cost.
5. The normalization of innovation quality effects through `theta` and the use of `y=kappa E` and `nu=rho/(2-rho^2)`.
6. The all-history price regularity condition is a technical globality restriction ensuring the intended price subgame remains valid for every feasible upstream history.

The first five items are not all innocuous for every possible extension, but Stage 7.5R does not claim robustness to replacing them. They are not the economic statement of the mechanism itself.

## 5. Alternative formulation survival

The mechanism, but not the full policy theorem, survives the verified general concave-technology formulation.

For any differentiable increasing strictly concave `g`, the private allocation is globally nonincreasing in scope and the coordinated symmetric allocation is globally nondecreasing. Strict opposite signs hold on intervals where the corresponding optima are interior. Thus the **directional appropriability mechanism is not created by the quadratic functional form**.

What does **not** survive as a generic claim is strict concavity or uniqueness of the induced policy objective under arbitrary small `C^2` perturbations. Accordingly, the Stage 8R freeze must separate:

- general mechanism robustness: retained;
- global selective-policy uniqueness robustness: not claimed.

This distinction is sufficient for the Stage 7.5 full-paper test, but it materially limits the appropriate journal-level claim.

## 7. Closest-paper distinction

The prior-stage literature boundary must remain narrow. Existing work already shows that compatibility can affect endogenous product design, that compatibility and standards can alter innovation incentives, that partial standardization can be optimal, and that firms allocate scarce innovative effort across activities. The surviving distinction is the **whole-game/result combination**: a regulator chooses a continuous standardization scope before firms allocate a fixed R&D capacity between a common layer and a proprietary layer; broader scope enters private and coordinated R&D incentives with opposite signs because common innovation strengthens rivals; and this endogenous portfolio response changes the policy conclusion from complete scope under fixed positive symmetric R&D to a unique interior scope under sufficiently strong rivalry. The contribution is the resulting policy reversal and threshold characterization, not any component statement such as “compatibility affects innovation” or “partial standardization may be optimal.”

No broader novelty statement is authorized at Stage 8R.

## 8. Welfare / organizational case

The welfare implication remains substantive after correcting the benchmark taxonomy.

The paper does not rely on a transfer-accounting artifact. Consumer surplus and producer surplus are derived from the same differentiated-demand system, and the regulator's welfare ranking is driven by real equilibrium quality changes. The central wedge is organizational/incentive based: the regulator values the cross-product usability of common innovation, while each firm discounts that margin because it strengthens a rival.

The strongest accurate comparison is:

1. **Fixed positive symmetric R&D composition:** complete standardization is optimal.
2. **Coordinated symmetric-R&D benchmark with decentralized Bertrand pricing:** complete standardization is optimal.
3. **Decentralized endogenous R&D composition:** sufficiently strong rivalry yields a unique selective scope in the quadratic baseline.

This is weaker than a true first-best-versus-second-best theorem, but it still identifies why an apparently beneficial interoperability instrument can optimally be limited when the regulator cannot directly control firms' innovation composition.

## 10. Fatal / major referee risks carried forward

No fatal blocker remains for Stage 8R. Four material risks must be frozen rather than ignored:

1. **Functional-form scope of the policy theorem.** P4's global uniqueness is quadratic-baseline specific. The paper must not imply generic policy robustness.
2. **Fixed-capacity dependence.** The exact policy theorem is proved with fixed total R&D. The endogenous-total-R&D extension supports only the relative-return wedge.
3. **Benchmark strength.** The paper no longer has a verified unconstrained first-best theorem. Any use of “first best” in Stage 10R is prohibited unless a separate rollback and proof are authorized.
4. **Result-level novelty is narrow.** The contribution must be positioned as the scope → endogenous portfolio → selective-policy threshold chain, not as discovering that standards affect innovation.

These risks lower the ceiling relative to the pre-Astra manuscript but do not justify terminating the full-paper route.

## 11. Full-paper value assessment

The repaired paper remains more than a parameter exercise for three reasons.

First, the policy instrument changes an endogenous strategic allocation margin rather than merely shifting demand or a payoff parameter. Second, the model produces a sharp contrast between the direct benefit of broader scope and the induced private retreat from common innovation. Third, the resulting policy theorem has a nontrivial threshold and a global uniqueness characterization in the baseline region.

The cost of the Astra repair is that the paper can no longer lean on a broad first-best contrast or generic curvature robustness. Its value therefore rests on mechanism clarity, exact threshold characterization, and disciplined contribution positioning.
