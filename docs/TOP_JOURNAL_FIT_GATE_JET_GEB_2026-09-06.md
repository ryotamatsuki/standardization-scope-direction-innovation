## 3. Current frozen contribution

The active v2 architecture is:

1. a regulator chooses continuous standardization scope `b`;
2. firms allocate fixed R&D capacity `E` between common-layer R&D `x_i` and proprietary R&D `E-x_i`;
3. firms subsequently compete in differentiated Bertrand prices;
4. greater scope makes common innovation more transferable to rivals and lowers its private relative return;
5. private common R&D therefore declines with scope in the quadratic baseline;
6. for general increasing strictly concave innovation technology, the private allocation is globally nonincreasing and the coordinated symmetric allocation globally nondecreasing, with strictness only on interior segments;
7. fixed positive symmetric R&D implies complete standardization;
8. coordinated symmetric R&D with decentralized Bertrand pricing also implies complete standardization;
9. decentralized endogenous R&D composition in the quadratic baseline yields a unique interior standardization scope iff rivalry exceeds the frozen threshold `bar_nu(y)`.

The exact policy-uniqueness theorem is intentionally quadratic-baseline specific.

## 4. JET collision test

The strongest reason not to generalize SSDI mechanically is the existing general direction-of-innovation literature.

Kevin A. Bryan and Jorge Lemus (2017), *The Direction of Innovation*, Journal of Economic Theory 172, 247–272, develops a general model in which a fixed number of researchers are allocated across research projects and studies directional distortions generated by racing and underappropriation. It explicitly asks how innovation policies affect the direction of research.

Source:
`https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022053117300972`

SSDI is distinct because the policy variable is mandatory standardization scope, the common/proprietary portfolio is embedded in differentiated-product competition, and the result is a policy reversal from complete scope under fixed/coordinated symmetric R&D to selective scope under decentralized R&D composition.

But abstraction toward

`policy-controlled spillover exposure -> fixed research capacity -> underappropriation -> innovation direction`

moves SSDI closer to Bryan–Lemus. Thus **generality is not automatically novelty-enhancing** here.

A second relevant JET benchmark is Acemoglu, Gancia, and Zilibotti (2012), *Competing Engines of Growth: Innovation and Standardization*, which studies a dynamic growth model where innovation and standardization interact. The mechanism is different, but it means the broad pairing of standardization and innovation is already established at JET level.

Source:
`https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022053110001262`

Accordingly, a JET upgrade is worthwhile only if it produces a genuinely new general theorem rather than merely removing the standards interpretation from the present model.

## 5. JET architecture test

### Current fit

`LOW`

The present paper has a clear economic mechanism and a sharp baseline result, but its strongest general statement is weak monotone comparative statics. The selective-policy threshold and uniqueness result depends on the quadratic baseline.

That architecture is not yet a general-interest theory paper in which the standards application is secondary to a broadly reusable theorem.

### Minimum credible JET upgrade

A credible JET version would need a general object such as:

`policy-controlled externality exposure -> endogenous allocation between transferable and privately appropriable activities`.

At minimum it would need new primitive-level results such as:

1. a general allocation theorem establishing the private/social directional wedge under decreasing/increasing differences;
2. a nontrivial general policy theorem showing when a policy with a positive direct effect is optimally incomplete because of the endogenous allocation response;
3. primitive conditions for interior policy existence, multiplicity, or uniqueness that do not merely restate the quadratic polynomial calculation;
4. economically interpretable curvature, elasticity, or cross-partial restrictions;
5. a novelty distinction showing the theorem is not a reformulation or special case of Bryan–Lemus or adjacent innovation-direction theory;
6. technical standards appearing as an application of the general theorem rather than defining the theorem.

### Why this is not a bounded repair

The post-Astra audit already showed that generic `C^2` perturbations do not preserve policy-objective strict concavity/uniqueness and that general concave technology yields only weak global monotonicity with interior strictness.

Therefore the JET transformation cannot be achieved by replacing the quadratic `g` with a generic concave `g`. It requires discovery of a new theorem architecture and a new novelty audit.

Upgrade classification:

`MAJOR THEORY REDESIGN / SEPARATE-PAPER CANDIDATE`

### JET decision

`NO-GO AS AN IN-PROJECT GENERALIZATION`

Sequel potential:

`HIGH, CONDITIONAL ON A NEW GENERAL POLICY THEOREM`

A suitable separate-project working architecture is:

**Policy-Controlled Spillovers and the Endogenous Allocation of Scarce Innovative Effort**.

If pursued, that project should start at Stage 0 rather than modify SSDI v2.

## 6. GEB architecture test

### Current fit

`VERY LOW`

The present model is a sequential game, but the central R&D allocation response is not generated by a rich strategic best-response system. The frozen private problem reduces to

`max_x (1-nu b) g(x) + g(E-x)`,

so the marginal condition determining a firm's R&D composition does not depend directly on the rival's contemporaneous R&D choice. The game is a vehicle for the IO policy mechanism rather than the contribution itself.

GEB's own stated standard is advancement of game theory or its applications. Historical R&D papers in GEB that are useful architecture benchmarks typically obtain a broader strategic result: for example, Amir and Wooders (2000) use submodularity to obtain a general R&D-game analysis; Amir, Evstigneev, and Wooders (2003) generalize noncooperative/cooperative R&D with endogenous spillovers; Baye and Hoppe (2003) establish strategic equivalence across classes of games.

These benchmarks imply that simply calling SSDI a game or adding one robustness exercise would not make it GEB-native.

### Minimum credible GEB upgrade

A credible GEB transformation would likely need at least one genuinely new strategic object, such as:

- R&D best responses that depend nontrivially on rival R&D decisions;
- endogenous spillover or compatibility choices by firms;
- endogenous specialization into innovator/imitator roles;
- network or coalition formation over common technological layers;
- a strategic-equivalence, supermodularity/submodularity, potential-game, equilibrium-selection, or design theorem of independent game-theoretic interest;
- an `n`-player characterization whose strategic structure remains interesting after deleting the standards application.

Those changes create a different game and would overlap conceptually with separate standards-coalition research rather than strengthen the current clean SSDI mechanism.

Upgrade classification:

`MAJOR GAME REDESIGN / DIFFERENT PAPER IDENTITY`

### GEB decision

`NO-GO AS AN IN-PROJECT GENERALIZATION`

Sequel potential:

`MODERATE, BUT LOWER THAN THE JET ROUTE`
