## 2. Surviving contribution to position

The journal-selection object is fixed:

`regulator-selected continuous standardization scope -> fixed-capacity common/proprietary R&D portfolio -> appropriability response -> complete-to-selective policy reversal -> exact rivalry threshold in the quadratic baseline`.

The paper does not claim novelty for standards affecting innovation, endogenous innovation direction, underappropriation, or partial standardization by themselves.

The exact policy threshold matters because the adverse portfolio response alone does not imply selective scope: below the rivalry threshold complete standardization remains optimal.

## 3. Current journal evidence

### 3.1 International Journal of Industrial Organization — PRIMARY

Current official scope states that IJIO aims at full coverage of theoretical and empirical questions in industrial organization, including strategic behavior, market structure, technological change, regulation, antitrust, and productivity, and explicitly encourages theoretical work.

Recent audience-fit evidence is unusually strong:

- Llanes (2024), `Innovation Incentives in Technical Standards`, appears in IJIO and directly studies innovation incentives around technical standards, although through complementary-technology development, inclusion, patent pools, and price caps rather than standard scope and common/proprietary R&D portfolios.
- IJIO also published `R&D competition and the direction of innovation` (2022), showing that the journal's audience directly values endogenous innovation direction.
- A 2026 IJIO article, `Standard-setting and the incentives to innovate: Evidence from the IEEE patent policy update`, studies how standard-setting policy reallocates innovation incentives across firms.

This creates both the best fit and the most demanding novelty audience. The manuscript already addresses that risk by conceding the broad prior results and narrowing the contribution to the scope-to-portfolio-to-policy-reversal mechanism and threshold.

**Assessment:** strongest substantive fit; first submission should be IJIO.

**Desk/referee risk:** medium-high, driven by the narrowness of the incremental result and the quadratic dependence of the exact policy theorem, not by scope mismatch.

### 3.2 The Journal of Industrial Economics — STRETCH

The official scope welcomes applied oligopoly theory, product differentiation and technical change, regulation and technology policy. It explicitly welcomes theoretical papers but says they should highlight implications for policy and/or empirical analysis.

Recent evidence shows that JIE continues to publish theory involving standards and R&D, including the 2024 article `Coupling Information Disclosure with a Quality Standard in R&D Contests`.

The paper fits the subject matter, but JIE is a harder editorial target for this manuscript because Stage 11 classifies the contribution as `DISTINCT BUT NARROW`, while the exact selective-scope theorem remains quadratic-baseline-specific. The paper has policy implications and observable empirical predictions, but it does not yet provide evidence or a general theorem that broadens the result beyond the current architecture.

**Assessment:** legitimate stretch, but not the efficient first submission.

**Desk/referee risk:** high.

### 3.3 Review of Industrial Organization — REALISTIC FALLBACK

Recent RIO output demonstrates a strong home for self-contained IO theory with policy implications. In 2026 it published `Horizontal Mergers and Innovation with Knowledge Spillovers`, a two-firm innovation model in which spillovers alter R&D incentives, as well as other compact theoretical IO papers on quality differentiation and disclosure.

The present paper's combination of differentiated Bertrand competition, innovation spillovers/transferability, endogenous R&D allocation, and policy fits this readership well. RIO places somewhat less pressure than IJIO/JIE on the result being a broad field-level theoretical contribution, while still requiring a clean IO mechanism and policy interpretation.

**Assessment:** strongest fallback if IJIO rejects on contribution breadth rather than correctness.

**Desk/referee risk:** medium.

### 3.4 Journal of Industry, Competition and Trade — SAFETY NET

JICT has published applied theoretical work on R&D and compatibility/network effects, including `R&D Investments in Markets with Network Effects` (2021), and its recent output covers competition and industrial/innovation policy.

The paper is technically stronger and more formal than much of the journal's policy-facing material, so fit is acceptable but the readership is less tightly centered on technical-standards IO theory than IJIO/RIO.

**Assessment:** credible safety journal if the top IO ladder rejects the contribution as too narrow.

**Desk/referee risk:** low-to-medium on scope, with some risk that the paper appears overly specialized relative to the journal's broader policy orientation.

## 6. Primary-target rationale: IJIO

IJIO is selected because all four dimensions align simultaneously:

1. **Field identity:** the paper is unambiguously industrial organization.
2. **Mechanism audience:** differentiated Bertrand competition, innovation incentives, spillovers/transferability, and standards are core IJIO objects.
3. **Recent revealed fit:** IJIO has recently published both technical-standards innovation theory and standards/innovation empirical work.
4. **Contribution scale:** the paper is `DISTINCT BUT NARROW`; IJIO can publish focused IO mechanisms without requiring a general economic-theory theorem, whereas JIE is a less forgiving first target for this exact result set.

The fact that Llanes (2024) appears in IJIO is not a reason to avoid the journal. It is a reason to make the distinction unusually explicit in the introduction and cover letter: Llanes studies development/inclusion/licensing institutions around complementary standard technologies; this paper studies regulator-chosen continuous scope, cross-product transferability, and endogenous common/proprietary portfolio reallocation.

## 9. Pre-specified submission sequence

1. Submit to **International Journal of Industrial Organization**.
2. If rejected primarily for contribution breadth / insufficient generality but no correctness failure is identified, retarget to **Review of Industrial Organization** rather than trying to enlarge the model reactively.
3. Use **The Journal of Industrial Economics** only if the author deliberately prefers a higher-risk stretch before RIO; it is not the default sequential fallback because a rejection from IJIO for narrowness predicts a similar or stronger breadth concern at JIE.
4. If RIO rejects the paper as too narrow for its audience, use **Journal of Industry, Competition and Trade** as the safety target.
5. Any referee report identifying a genuine theorem/equilibrium failure overrides this ladder and triggers workflow rollback.

This produces the operational default ladder:

`IJIO -> RIO -> JICT`,

with JIE retained as an optional stretch rather than the automatic second submission.
